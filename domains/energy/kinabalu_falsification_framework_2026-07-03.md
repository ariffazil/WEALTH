## 3. The Three Weakest Claims in v4 (per reviewer + GEOX)

### W1 — "Multicausal uplift" (geodynamics)
- **Why weak:** The five-mechanism list (extension + drip + delamination + isostasy + pluton) is correct but unweighted. No mass-balance model exists.
- **Strengthen by:** Coupled thermomechanical modelling (e.g. ASPECT-style), with Kinabalu granite as a buoyancy tracer.
- **Kill by:** Discovery that uplift rate is uniform (single-cause) rather than pulsing (multicausal).

### W2 — "Dangerous Grounds jammed the subduction at 15 Ma" (collision timing)
- **Why weak:** The exact moment of jamming is not directly dated; it is inferred from the chronosequence and stratigraphic change (Gomantong → Labang transition).
- **Strengthen by:** Direct dating of the ophiolite-collision contact (Ar-Ar on hornblende, U-Pb on zircon from cross-cutting dikes).
- **Kill by:** Discovery that the collision is older (Eocene?) or younger (<10 Ma), with continuous subduction signature.

### W3 — "Jurassic carbonate décollement at 12–21 km" (deep detachment interpretation)
- **Why weak:** Reviewer (ChatGPT) explicitly flagged this as **unproven hypothesis**. Sabah offshore literature supports Oligocene–Miocene carbonates more strongly.
- **Strengthen by:** Carbonate age dating from a deep well, Vp profile, or xenolith sample.
- **Kill by:** Discovery of granitic/metamorphic basement at that depth, OR absence of high-reflectivity horizon, OR identification as Oligocene–Miocene instead.
